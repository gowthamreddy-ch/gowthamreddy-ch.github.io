REELLAB MODEL AND EVALUATION CARD
October 7, 2026

PURPOSE
  Investigate whether a locally trained collaborative recommender improves
  held-out movie ranking over a popularity baseline, and how recommendations
  change with a sparse profile. Intended for an educational research demo.

METHOD
  Fit a global mean, shrinkage-regularized user/item biases, and 12-dimensional
  user/item factors by alternating least squares. Use 12 iterations and
  compare regularization values 0.05 and 0.15 on validation NDCG@10.
  Select 0.15. Fold each evaluated profile into the same frozen item factors
  used by the interactive application. Zero-history users receive popularity.

PROTOCOL
  Sort all ratings by timestamp and split globally at the 80th/90th percentile
  boundaries. Equal timestamps stay together. Neither validation nor test
  labels are fitted. For final ranking, exclude movies rated in training or
  validation. Candidates include all movies observed in training, without
  sampled negatives. Relevant items have held-out ratings of at least 4.
  Users without eligible positive test items do not enter ranking averages.
  Coverage reports relevant test items excluded because they were unseen in
  training. Details, split sizes and cutoffs are in RESULTS.json.

RESULTS
  Full-history personalized NDCG@10: 0.193307.
  Popularity NDCG@10: 0.192905.
  Paired user-bootstrap 95% interval for the difference: [-0.007315, 0.007414].
  There is no established improvement over popularity on this experiment.
  Both methods hit at least one observed relevant item in 66.7% of the
  147 eligible test users. 95 of those users had no training history and
  received the same popularity fallback. This strongly affects the aggregate.

  With at most three training ratings per user, personalized NDCG@10 is
  0.181977 versus 0.192905 for popularity. Its paired interval is approximately
  [-0.023351, -0.000097]. This sparse-profile setup performs worse on this split.
  The sparse experiment selects the latest training ratings. It is not a
  simulation of the demo visitor choosing a few favorite films.

  Uncertainty is estimated with 1,000 paired user bootstrap samples, seed 71.
  It describes user resampling on this fixed split, not uncertainty across
  datasets, eras, model families, or live deployments. Differences involving
  the final test set have not been used to retune the model.

LIMITATIONS
  - The data and film catalog are historical; new releases are absent.
  - Unrated items are unknown, not negatives. Observed-feedback selection
    bias limits the interpretation of ranking scores.
  - Cold users receive popularity; cold items cannot be ranked by this model.
  - A small self-selected profile may produce weak recommendations.
  - Genre-diversity reranking is available for exploration but has no claimed
    accuracy benefit and is not included in the reported model comparison.
  - A similarity explanation is descriptive, not a causal attribution.
  - No online user study, business uplift, production scale, or fairness
    across demographic groups is claimed. Demographic data is unused.

NEXT EXPERIMENTS
  Define a fresh validation design before adding content features for cold
  items/users. Compare sparse-profile elicitation strategies, add warm/cold
  slices, and evaluate ranking versus diversity at predeclared settings.
  Preserve the original result as a baseline; do not overwrite its history
  with selectively chosen results. A future user study would require actual
  participants and its own protocol.

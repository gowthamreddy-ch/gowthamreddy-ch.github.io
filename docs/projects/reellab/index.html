Applied AI · Film studies

ReelLab — Learn to think like a filmmaker

Working prototype

A filmmaking study space with sourced craft examples, phone exercises, filmmaker exploration, and live movie and series taste discovery.

Current local version · 12 October 2026. Built for aspiring filmmakers who want to connect what they watch with something they can try.

This page presents the working application through screenshots and engineering evidence. The interactive app requires a Python server and provider credentials; contact me for a demonstration.

01 / Walkthrough

From a film to a practical lesson

01

Choose the title, then the question

Search movies and series or browse a filmmaker’s credits. Exact media type, catalog ID and release year keep similarly named people and titles separate. Read the premise, choose a study focus and set a spoiler preference.

02

Turn watching into practice

Explore camera, lighting, lenses, editing, sound or action choreography. Craft research looks for documented production methods, checks the supporting passages and builds a three-step phone exercise. When evidence is insufficient, the study labels general practice instead of asserting a film-specific example.

03

Follow your taste, then shuffle

Taste Lab searches TMDB movies and series beyond the original historical dataset. Rate titles, choose a main genre and an optional secondary preference, then shuffle fresh picks. Increasing variety explores a broader pool; a current round excludes rated and previously shown titles.

02 / Engineering

What supports the experience

Problem and scope

A small recommendation list was not enough for learning filmmaking. ReelLab connects broad title discovery with questions about story and craft, then makes the lesson approachable with a phone or basic camera.

Implementation

Built a Python API, a responsive HTML/CSS/JavaScript interface, catalog identity resolution, filmmaker credit browsing, source retrieval and review, generated exercises, saved notebooks and a separate Taste Lab.

Research flow

Resolve the exact title and its alternative names, search for practitioner sources, retrieve supporting passages, and review attribution before presenting an example. Phone lessons are original teaching adaptations. Independent model review helps catch errors but does not guarantee accuracy.

Catalog and taste ranking

TMDB discovery and paginated search replace the old catalog restriction. Ranking combines community quality, popularity, genre affinity and related-title signals. The main genre is required when selected; the second is preferred. Scores shown are TMDB community ratings, not predicted personal ratings.

Recorded checks

The Taste Lab change passed 40 targeted unit/regression checks. A live test returned 40 distinct movies across four batches. Browser checks covered movie and series search, rating import and persistence, genre preferences, shuffle, separate-tab navigation and layouts at 1366, 768 and 390 pixels.

Evidence and limits

Earlier craft-pipeline checks produced sourced examples and accepted phone lessons for Stranger Things, RRR and both Baahubali films. These are observed checks, not a general accuracy benchmark. Research can take several minutes or withhold examples when sources are sparse, blocked or ambiguous.

Credentials and saved data

API credentials stay in local server configuration. Browser-saved ratings are sent to ReelLab’s server for ranking; study context is sent to the research provider. The public portfolio contains reviewed screenshots and aggregate evidence. Source is preserved privately; credentials, logs, caches and user records are excluded.

Historical model, separate evidence

The original MovieLens 100K experiment remains documented: NDCG@10 was 0.1933 for personalization versus 0.1929 for popularity; the paired 95% interval for the difference included zero. It did not establish improvement. These historical results do not evaluate the current TMDB taste ranker.

Network Incident Triage Assistant

Working prototype

A working incident-triage assistant that connects sanitized logs, topology and retrieved runbooks to a source-linked hypothesis and a reviewed set of read-only checks.

Working Evidence Lab rebuild with synthetic vendor-style incidents. The screenshot is a labeled saved example. Live model results were recorded September 29; the assistant never executes network commands.

Explore the screenshots, walkthrough and measured results below. The interactive application currently runs locally; contact me for a demonstration.

01 / Walkthrough

System walkthrough

A sequence of workflows to explain the project.

01

Connect logs and topology

Load a synthetic Cisco IOS, Juniper Junos, Arista EOS, or FRRouting-style scenario. Inspect its logs and topology, then use the saved example or request a live draft. Deterministic keyword overlap retrieves relevant local runbooks.

02

Review a grounded hypothesis

Inspect cited log IDs, runbook references, allowed read-only checks, and uncertainties. Review is required before export. Approval records review of the draft; it neither runs commands nor authorizes a network change.

02 / Engineering

Engineering evidence

Problem and scope

Incident diagnosis requires relating noisy logs to expected topology and operating guidance. This local assistant drafts a traceable hypothesis for review instead of acting on a network.

My role and contribution

Implemented incident preparation and masking, keyword-based runbook retrieval, structured live drafts, validation of source IDs and allowlisted checks, a synthetic scenario selector, review controls, and export. A hand-authored saved example supports offline demonstration.

Architecture and data flow

The Python service prepares the logs and topology, ranks a small local runbook corpus, and passes selected evidence to the OpenAI Responses API. Validators constrain references and check IDs. The browser exposes sources and uncertainty beside the hypothesis.

Technology choices and tradeoffs

Use transparent keyword retrieval for a small corpus. Separate diagnosis-label agreement from evidence support. Constrain checks to a vetted read-only catalog and leave execution outside the assistant. Version prompts and retain evaluation metadata.

Results and evaluation

The September 29 saved live report matched 20/20 synthetic scenario diagnosis labels, with 5/5 for each of four vendor styles. Top-1 and top-3 retrieval each matched 5/5 on a five-case Cisco comparison subset; these small results do not establish a benefit from extra retrieval. The final validation record rechecked all 30 retained triage outputs. Today’s browser inspection confirmed the labeled saved draft and supporting evidence.

Limitations and next steps

Synthetic vendor-style logs are not production outages. Diagnosis-code matches and valid references do not prove that every statement is supported. Independent human semantic review and the planned incomplete/conflicting-evidence study remain future work. Recorded live evaluations date to September 29, 2026; fresh API availability and billing were not retested for this portfolio update. Saved examples are hand-authored and labeled. Data and network devices are synthetic. No customer study, production performance, physical-device recovery, or public deployment is claimed. Source stays private.

03 / Research roadmap

Next investigation

Proposed extension

Research question

How does the assistant’s behavior change when incident evidence is incomplete or conflicting?

Planned method

Compare a simple baseline with a versioned assistant on held-out, sanitized incident cases with verified diagnoses. Include complete, incomplete, and conflicting evidence; define a scoring rubric before reviewing outputs.

What I would measure

  • Agreement with verified diagnoses
  • Recommendations supported by the supplied evidence
  • Appropriate clarification requests or deferrals
  • Time to a human-reviewed draft

This is a proposed extension. No findings from this study are claimed. Case selection, rubric, and baseline still need to be finalized.
